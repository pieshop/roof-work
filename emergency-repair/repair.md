## Objective of Emergency Repair

The brief is:

**Active leak → diagnose cause → minimum appropriate repair → make roof watertight.**

This is separate from the main roof/chimney/gable project.

A proper local repair to slipped, broken or missing slates is fine. The intention is not to insist on a temporary repair if the affected slates can be repaired properly.

However, do not make a decision about:

- Complete reroofing
- Stripping the whole roof
- Re-slating the whole roof
- Replacing all battens
- Installing a new membrane
- Other substantial roof works

until the roof has been assessed as part of the wider contractor process.

## If Asked What I Think Is Wrong

> I’m not sure whether they’re missing, broken or slipped slates. I can see the gaps clearly from inside the loft, but I’d rather have you establish what’s actually causing it.

Do not assume that the visible gap is necessarily the exact point where the water is entering. Water may enter higher up the roof and travel along a slate, batten or timber before dripping into the loft.

## If Asked About the Wider Roof

> I’m actually in the process of getting the roof assessed as part of some other work on the house, so for the moment I’m really looking for a local repair to stop the water coming in. I don’t want to make a decision about reroofing until I’ve had the whole roof properly assessed.

Do not initially introduce the possible option of stripping and re-slating using the original slates. That is relevant to the main project but could unnecessarily steer an emergency repair conversation towards a much larger job.

## Repair From Inside the Loft

The loft provides useful access for:

- Locating the areas where water is appearing
- Seeing gaps/daylight through the slate covering
- Inspecting battens and roof timbers
- Looking for evidence of water tracking from another location
- Helping identify the corresponding external roof area

However, a proper repair to slipped, broken or missing slates would normally need to be carried out **externally**.

An internal patch using silicone, expanding foam, felt or similar material should not be treated as the permanent repair.

If necessary, an internal measure may be useful temporarily to manage incoming water until the exterior can safely be repaired.

## Information to Obtain After Repair

Ask what they found:

- How many slates were affected?
- Were they missing, broken or slipped?
- Why had they failed?
- Were the slate fixings failing?
- Was there evidence of nail sickness?
- What condition were the battens in?
- Did they see evidence that this is an isolated problem or something more widespread?
- Were replacement slates used?
- If so, were they new or reclaimed?
- How were the repaired/replacement slates fixed?
- Did they see any other areas requiring urgent attention?

Keep any before/after photographs provided by the roofer.

The findings should then be incorporated into the assessment of the three longer-term roof options:

1. Repair/overhaul the existing roof
2. Strip and re-slate using the sound original slates, with new membrane and battens
3. Complete reroof using replacement slate

## Important

The discovery of active water ingress is significant new evidence about the condition of the roof, but it does **not by itself establish that the complete roof needs replacing**.

The important question is why the gaps have developed.

A small number of individually damaged or displaced slates may simply require local repair.

Multiple slipped slates caused by widespread deterioration of the original fixings would be more significant and would strengthen the case for considering stripping and re-slating the roof.
