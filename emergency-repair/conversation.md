If they ask what you think is wrong:

I’m not sure whether they’re missing, broken or slipped slates. I can see the gaps clearly from inside the loft, but I’d rather have you establish what’s actually causing it.

If they ask about the wider condition of the roof:

I’m actually in the process of getting the roof assessed as part of some other work on the house, so for the moment I’m really looking for a local repair to stop the water coming in. I don’t want to make a decision about reroofing until I’ve had the whole roof properly assessed.

If they can come out, I’d ask:

Great. Is there a call-out charge, and can you give me an idea of how you charge for this sort of repair?

And before finishing:

If you find that it’s more than a few damaged or slipped slates, could you show me what you’ve found before doing anything beyond the immediate repair?

One thing I wouldn’t say

I wouldn’t initially tell them “I think the roof might need stripping and re-slating with the original slates.” That’s useful for your main contractor discussions, but it unnecessarily plants the idea of a much larger job with someone you’re calling for an emergency repair.

For this call, active leak → diagnose → minimum proper repair → make watertight is exactly the brief.
