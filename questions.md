# Contractor Visit Questions

> **Visit approach:** Let the contractor inspect the property and explain their assessment first. Start with the open-ended overall assessment question before discussing possible solutions. Use the remaining questions to fill gaps rather than working through them as a questionnaire.

## Core Questions

These are the key questions to get answered during every contractor visit.

- Having inspected everything, what do you think are the main problems and what work do you recommend?
- Does what you have seen broadly agree with the structural engineer's report?
- Do you agree that the chimney should be reduced/removed, and how would you approach it?
- What do you think needs to be done to repair the bulging/damaged gable wall and provide the additional wall ties?
- How would you reinstate proper support to the affected purlin?
- Given that this is a circa-1900 house with what appears to be the original slate roof, what condition do you think the roof is in and what would you recommend: overhaul, strip and re-slate using the original slates, or replacement with new slate? Why?
- If we overhaul the existing roof rather than strip it, what sort of further service life would you realistically expect?
- If we stripped the roof, what percentage of the original slates do you think would be reusable, and how would the price compare between reusing them and supplying completely new natural slate?
- Are you prepared to take responsibility for the complete package, including any specialist subcontractors?
- Is there anything that requires further structural engineer input, Building Control or other approval before work starts?
- What are the main uncertainties that could cause the price to increase once work starts?
- Will you provide an itemised written quote covering the complete scope, including scaffolding, waste and VAT?

## Loft Inspection

Loft ladder/access installed before contractor visits. Loft boarding deliberately postponed until the roof/structural works have been assessed.

Ask contractor to inspect the loft, particularly:

- Internal face of the gable/flank wall and visible defective/missing masonry
- Purlin bearing/support at the affected wall
- General condition and alignment of the purlins and roof timbers
- Any evidence of movement, distortion or previous structural repairs
- Areas where daylight is visible through the roof
- Evidence of current or historic water ingress
- Underside/condition of slates and battens
- Condition of the roof timbers, particularly any evidence of historic water penetration
- Chimney structure and surrounding timbers where accessible

### Before Loft Boarding

- Will the proposed roof/gable/purlin work require access from inside the loft?
- Are there areas that should remain permanently accessible?
- Is there anything I should repair or alter before having the loft boarded?
- Would the proposed boarding interfere with ventilation or inspection of the roof structure?

## Overall Assessment

- Having inspected it, what do you think are the main problems?
- Does what you can see agree with the RICS survey and structural engineer's report?
- Is there anything you think the reports have missed?
- Which work do you consider urgent, and which could reasonably wait?
- Do you see any areas that need opening up before the full extent of the work can be established?

## Chimney

- Do you agree that the rear flank chimney should be reduced/removed?
- To what level would you take the chimney down?
- How would you close and weatherproof the remaining flues?
- How would you make good the roof where the chimney currently passes through it?
- Do you foresee any structural issues when removing the chimney?
- Would Building Control need to be involved?

## Gable / Flank Wall

- What do you think has caused the bulging and damaged masonry?
- Does the affected masonry need local repair, or does part of the wall need rebuilding?
- How extensive do you think the masonry repairs will be?
- Can you undertake this work yourselves?
- Do you agree with the structural engineer's recommendation for additional wall ties?
- Would you install the wall ties yourselves or use a specialist?
- Is there anything that needs to be done to prevent the problem recurring?

## Purlin / Roof Structure

- What do you think needs to be done to reinstate proper support to the purlin?
- Is this a localised repair or is there evidence of a wider roof-structure problem?
- Can you undertake the structural timber/purlin work yourselves?
- Would you want further input from the structural engineer before doing this work?

## Existing Slate Roof

The Level 2 survey describes the house as constructed circa 1900. The roof is believed to be the original slate roof. There is no roofing underlay beneath the existing slates.

- What condition do you think the original slates are in?
- Are the slates themselves generally capable of being retained?
- Is there evidence that the roof is becoming nail-sick or that the slate fixings are failing?
- What condition are the battens in?
- What condition are the rafters, purlins and other roof timbers in?
- Roughly how much of the roof currently needs repair?
- Can slipped/loose slates be refixed, or will significant numbers need replacing?
- Are there any problems with the ridge, valleys, abutments or flashings?
- What do you think is causing the areas where daylight is visible from the loft?
- Is there evidence of current or historic water ingress?
- Is the absence of roofing underlay causing any actual problems with the roof in its current condition?
- If I only overhaul the roof now, what sort of remaining life would you realistically expect from it?

## If Stripping / Reroofing Is Suggested

- Why do you recommend stripping the roof rather than repairing/overhauling it in place?
- Is the problem primarily the slates, their fixings, the battens, or something else?
- What percentage of the original slates do you think could realistically be salvaged and reused?
- If the original slates are sound, would you recommend re-slating with them over new membrane and battens?
- Would matching reclaimed slate be available to make up any shortfall?
- What type of breathable roofing membrane would you use?
- Would all the battens be replaced?
- Would stripping the roof expose any other work that you expect might be necessary?
- Would any changes to insulation or roof ventilation be required?
- Could you quote separately for overhaul, re-slating with the original slates, and reroofing with new slate?
- How would the cost of reusing the original slate compare with supplying completely new natural slate?
- What would be the additional cost of doing this while the scaffolding is already present?
- What guarantee would you provide if the roof were re-slated using the original slates?
- What guarantee would you provide if new slate were used?

## Rainwater / Damp

- Are the gutters or downpipes contributing to the dampness of the flank wall?
- Are the gutter falls and outlets adequate?
- Where are the downpipes currently discharging?
- Do any changes need to be made to the gullies or drainage?

## Responsibility for the Complete Job

- Are you prepared to take responsibility for the complete package of works?
- Which parts would your own team undertake?
- Which parts, if any, would you subcontract?
- Who would manage and take responsibility for subcontractors?
- Would you arrange and manage the scaffolding?
- Would you liaise directly with the structural engineer if questions arise?
- Do you think any further structural engineering input is required?
- Do you think Planning or Building Control approval is required for any part of the work?

## Quote

- Will you provide an itemised written quote?
- Can the quote separate the main elements of the work?
- Will scaffolding be separately identified?
- Will VAT be clearly shown?
- Will waste removal be included?
- Will the quote identify any provisional sums or items that cannot be priced until work starts?
- What circumstances could cause the final price to increase?
- If additional defects are uncovered, will you agree the cost with me before carrying out additional work?
- If significant roof work is recommended, can the quote distinguish between:
  1. Repair/overhaul of the existing roof
  2. Strip and re-slate using the sound original slates, with new membrane and battens
  3. Complete reroof using new slate

## Guarantee / Insurance

- What guarantee will you provide for the work?
- Are different parts of the work guaranteed for different periods?
- Is the guarantee insurance-backed?
- Do you carry public liability insurance?
- Can you provide details of the insurance with the quote?

## Practicalities

- When could you realistically start?
- Roughly how long would the work take?
- Would the house remain watertight throughout the work?
- Will the scaffold require access to neighbouring property?
- Will you need access through the house or loft?
- Will a skip be required, and where would you expect to put it?
- How do you normally structure payments?
- Do you require a deposit?

## Before They Leave

- Is there anything you have seen today that particularly concerns you?
- Is there anything you would recommend doing differently from the structural engineer's proposed approach?
- Is there anything else you think I should know before comparing quotes?
