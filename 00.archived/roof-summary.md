# Roof Summary

## TLDR

Both reports agree the main roof issue is structural and urgent: the rear flank chimney is leaning inward, and the high-level gable/flank wall brickwork is bulging/defective, with missing inner-leaf masonry in the loft. The structural report attributes this mainly to lack of chimney support and failed/insufficient wall ties, and recommends lowering/removing the chimney below roof level, repairing masonry, adding wall ties, and making good the roof.

## Reports Reviewed

- `00.reports/level-2-survey.pdf` - RICS Level 2 survey by Bramptons, inspection date `22/10/2024`.
- `00.reports/structural-assessment-report.pdf` - Structural assessment by White & Lloyd, site visit `05/11/2024`, issued `11/11/2024`.

## Main Roof Findings

The RICS survey gives urgent `Condition Rating 3` to:

- `D1 Chimney stacks`
- `D2 Roof coverings`
- `D3 Rainwater pipes and gutters`
- `D4 Main walls`
- `E1 Roof structure`
- `E5 Fireplaces, chimney breasts and flues`

The most serious finding is the rear flank chimney stack. It is described as leaning substantially inward. The RICS survey says remedial works will be necessary and likely involve removal or substantial reduction in height.

The gable/flank wall is also a serious issue. Both reports describe bulging/defective brickwork at high level near the roof ridge. In the loft, missing, cracked and bulging brickwork was seen, including missing masonry beneath/near the front roof purlin.

The front roof purlin is noted in the RICS report as not adequately supported because brickwork has come away. That means the roof repair is not just cosmetic roof covering work; it intersects with structural masonry repairs.

There is water ingress evidence: water staining under the ridge line, daylight visible at the junction between the main roof and rear addition, damp around chimneys, and moisture ingress near the flank chimney.

The main slate roof is over 120 years old and described as being at the end of its economic lifespan. The RICS report says that if retained, it will need a substantial overhaul and regular maintenance.

## Structural Report Conclusions

White & Lloyd identify two principal structural defects:

- Rear flank chimney stack leaning substantially inward.
- End gable/flank wall bulging at the top near the roof ridge.

Their likely causes are:

- Chimney: lack of support for approximately `1.8m` of chimney stack above roof level. They describe this as likely a construction defect and note other nearby properties appear to have dealt with similar issues by lowering/removing this chimney.
- Gable/flank wall: failed, corroded, or insufficient wall ties between the inner and outer masonry leaves. Missing inner-leaf masonry in the loft means there are also areas with no wall ties present.

Their recommended works are:

- Reduce the rear flank chimney stack to below roof level or to a new level within the internal loft space.
- Install new roof finishes where the chimney currently penetrates the roof.
- Cap/cover the reduced chimney stack to prevent moisture or debris entering disused flues.
- Replace defective or missing masonry to the gable/flank wall.
- Install new wall ties, ideally at minimum `2.5 wall ties per square metre`, staggered between brick courses.
- Use scaffolding for safe access.

Their rough budget estimate for building works is `£6,553` including VAT, but this excludes design consultants, structural/architectural fees, planning/building control costs, and any wider roof overhaul beyond the specified defects.

## Other Roof-Related Issues

The RICS report also notes:

- General roof undulation and gaps/slates not sitting flush.
- Past roof repairs visible.
- Evidence of nail sickness/slipped or defective slates, especially to older slate areas.
- Broken/slipped slates to ancillary roofs.
- No roof lining under the roof.
- No roof vents.
- Dampness around chimneys.
- Possible water penetration near the front chimney and raised slates behind it.

Rainwater goods are also contributing to damp risk:

- Side gutters discharge directly to ground against the flank wall.
- A front downpipe was blocked with visible standing water.
- Shared front cast iron gutter/hopper needs to be kept clear and maintained.

## Practical Priority Order

1. Treat the rear flank chimney and gable/flank wall as the urgent structural work.
2. Get contractor quotes based on the structural report scope: scaffold, chimney reduction, roof infill/making good, masonry repairs, wall ties.
3. Confirm whether planning permission and/or building control approval is required before works.
4. Have the wider roof inspected/quoted separately because the RICS report suggests the roof covering itself is old and near end-of-life.
5. Address rainwater discharge/blockages because they are likely worsening damp and masonry deterioration.

## Key Risk

The immediate risk is not simply a bad roof; it is a combination of unsupported/leaning chimney, weakened/bulging gable masonry, compromised purlin support, water ingress, and an ageing slate roof. The structural report narrows the core structural repair, but the RICS report implies there may be broader roof maintenance or renewal costs beyond the `£6,553` budget estimate.
