## Useful Points From Sam Heath's 13/11/2024 Reply

- Urgency: Sam said the work was not urgent, but should be undertaken in the short/medium term.
- Chimney condition: the chimney breast appeared generally good apart from the leaning, although Sam could not observe all sides fully.
- Roof structure: from visual inspection, the roof structure appeared fine.
- Timber survey: the home buyer survey had recommended a timber defect survey because of water staining under the ridgeline; Sam said this might be useful for peace of mind.
- Design input: Sam said he would not anticipate the works requiring any design input.
- Planning: reduction in height of the chimney stack to below the roof finishes may fall under permitted development; if not, planning approval would typically cost around £258 + VAT.
- Building Control: Building Control approval may be required for covering the hole in the roof once the chimney has been removed; typical costs for works of this size were stated as around £300-£400 + VAT.
- Neighbour impact: expected impact on No. 64 was mainly scaffold feet near/opposite their front door entrance; with kickboards to prevent falling debris, impact should be minimal. Sam did not expect impact on No. 68 because scaffolding would be away from their property.
- Builder recommendations: White & Lloyd did not recommend specific builders, but suggested using the Federation of Master Builders website.

## Relevance To Current Decision

- The 2024 White & Lloyd follow-up tends to support a proportionate / staged approach rather than assuming a full structural rebuild must be designed before works start.
- It is relevant that Sam said no design input was anticipated, but this was based on the condition and visual access at that time.
- The current roof decision has changed: Stephen is now planning a full roof replacement because the roof is original and appears near end of life.
- South Thames / Roofcraft-style approach: reroof first, carry out limited purlin/support works and wall ties if needed, and stop for SE input if substantial/alarming structural issues are exposed.
- Auletta-style approach: allow for a more cautious gable wall rebuild / structural package from the outset, with longer programme and all-weather scaffold lid.
- The key question for White & Lloyd is whether their 2024 view still supports the staged roofer-led approach now that the roof is being replaced, or whether they would now recommend a repair design/specification before works start.

## Caveats

- The email chain is from November 2024, almost two years before the current contractor decision.
- Sam's roof-structure comment was based on visual inspection only.
- Active water ingress and visible daylight through the roof were observed later, on 08/09/2026.
- The White & Lloyd structural report itself remains the primary structural reference; the email chain helps interpret urgency/design-input expectations but does not replace the report.
