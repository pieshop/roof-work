## Key Differences And Risks

### Roof Covering

Stephen has decided to use slate rather than concrete tile.

Roofcraft quote #347 is for natural grey roof slates, but does not specify supplier/origin, size or grade. South Thames quote T90032 is for Grade A natural slates, 500x250mm, but does not specify supplier/origin. Both need slate specification clarified before acceptance.

### Building Control

There is a direct discrepancy:

- Roofcraft: Ben verbally said roof Building Control is not legally required, though Stephen could arrange it for documentation at around £350. He said an inspector would check loft insulation and require correction if not up to regulations.
- Jack Auletta: Matthew Carter verbally said on 16/09/2026 that roof replacement does not legally require Building Regulations sign-off.
- South Thames: Stephen Baker's email says replacement roof coverings require Building Control certification by law, but South Thames can self-certify under the Competent Person Scheme and issue the certificate at no additional cost.

This should be resolved before appointment. South Thames' quote is stronger on documented certification because it expressly includes the LABC certificate, but its legal position now conflicts with verbal views from both Roofcraft and Jack Auletta.

### Structural Scope Gap

Neither quote currently looks like a complete whole-package answer to `job-description.txt`.

Roofcraft is explicit that the side apex / wall-tie area is investigation only and remedial/structural works would be quoted separately after exposure. Ben verbally said it might require brick removal and rework if issues are structural, but he could not price it until assessed.

South Thames does not clearly include gable/flank structural masonry repair, wall ties, or the purlin bearing/support repair required by the structural report. The wording about 4x2 timber supports to the loft area may or may not address the purlin issue; this needs clarification.

This is now the largest cost risk. The live quote comparison is around £21k-£22k for slate reroof plus chimney reduction and limited/unclear structural items, not a final guaranteed price for the entire White & Lloyd structural package.

### Payment Terms

South Thames is materially more favourable on payment terms: payment due upon completion of works.

Roofcraft's verbal terms were 25% deposit on job commission, 25% halfway and 50% on completion. That is not automatically unacceptable, but it increases upfront exposure and should be documented clearly before acceptance.

### Guarantees And Insurance

South Thames' quote is stronger on paper: all work guaranteed for 15 years, insurance backed for 10, and insurance certificates available on request.

Roofcraft's quote states a 10-year guarantee for the new replacement roof. Ben verbally said the 10-year guarantee covers the roof and mentioned £2 million insurance cover. Written confirmation is still needed, especially for chimney, purlin brickwork and any structural elements.

## Current Assessment

South Thames remains stronger on paper for certification, guarantee and payment terms. It is also lower on headline price: £21,120 including VAT versus Roofcraft #347 at £21,948 including VAT before the stated £200 + VAT temporary repair deduction, or approximately £21,708 including VAT after that deduction if applied as expected.

Roofcraft remains stronger on known direct engagement, responsiveness, the completed temporary repair, and explicit investigation of the side apex / wall-tie area. Quote #347 now makes Roofcraft a fairer slate-to-slate comparison with South Thames, but it still excludes side apex/wall-tie remedial works and needs written clarification on slate specification, Building Control, guarantees, insurance and payment terms.

On current figures, the price gap between the two slate options is modest compared with the unresolved structural and rainwater scope. The decision should not be made on the £588-£828 headline difference alone.

Neither quote should be accepted as a complete whole-package quote until the structural masonry, wall-tie, purlin-support and rainwater/drainage scope is clarified in writing.

## Current Ranking On Paper

1. **South Thames on written terms:** lower headline price, completion-only payment, written LABC/certification inclusion, longer stated guarantee and insurance-backed guarantee.
2. **Roofcraft on engagement / continuity:** better direct engagement so far, temporary repair completed, explicit plan to expose/investigate side apex / wall-tie area, but weaker written terms and more unresolved confirmations.
3. **No clear winner on whole-package scope:** neither quote clearly includes the full structural gable/flank wall repair, wall-tie works, purlin bearing/support works, or rainwater goods/drainage corrections required by `job-description.txt`.

## Provisional Decision View

Do not choose solely on headline price; the totals are effectively comparable in the context of a £21k-£22k slate reroof package and potentially material excluded structural work.

If the priority is documented Building Control certification, longer written guarantee and favourable payment terms, South Thames currently looks stronger on paper.

If the priority is continuity with the contractor who has already inspected twice, completed the temporary repair, and is explicitly planning to expose/investigate the side apex/wall-tie area, Roofcraft remains a serious candidate. Quote #347 means Roofcraft can now be compared as a slate option, but written confirmations are still needed before fair acceptance.
